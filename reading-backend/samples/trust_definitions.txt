Trust is one of the most cited yet least consistently defined concepts in social theory, and the disagreement is not merely terminological: different definitions locate trust in different places, with different consequences for how institutions are meant to earn it. For Niklas Luhmann (1979), trust is fundamentally a mechanism for reducing social complexity. Modern life presents an overwhelming number of possible futures, and trust allows an actor to act as though only a limited range of those futures were relevant, without first calculating the probability of each. On this account, trust is a cognitive shortcut, exercised toward systems as readily as toward persons. Anthony Giddens (1990) locates trust differently, defining it as confidence in the reliability of a person or system in the face of contingent outcomes, confidence that reflects a 'leap into faith' which brushes aside the very risks it responds to. For Giddens, trust in modernity is increasingly directed at abstract expert systems rather than individuals, since most people lack the specialist knowledge to evaluate the systems they depend on directly. A third position, associated with Diego Gambetta (1988), treats trust as a subjective probability estimate: to trust another agent is to assign a sufficiently high probability that they will perform a beneficial or at least non-harmful action, high enough that one is willing to engage in some form of cooperation with them. Gambetta's account is narrower than Luhmann's and Giddens's in one respect and broader in another: narrower because it reduces trust to a calculable probability rather than a general orientation to uncertainty, broader because it applies equally well to trust between individuals, trust in institutions, and trust in non-human systems such as algorithms, without requiring any particular psychological state. These three accounts differ most sharply on whether trust is best understood as a form of calculation, a form of confidence, or a form of complexity reduction, and each implies a different research agenda for the empirical study of trust in digital platforms.
